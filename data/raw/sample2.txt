Coffee beans are actually the seeds found inside the fruit of the Coffea plant, commonly called coffee cherries.

The two most widely cultivated species of coffee are Coffea arabica (Arabica) and Coffea canephora (Robusta).

Arabica beans account for roughly 60% of world production and thrive at high altitudes between 1,000 and 2,000 meters above sea level.

Robusta beans grow well at lower elevations, contain nearly double the caffeine content of Arabica, and produce a thicker crema when brewed for espresso.

The region where coffee grows globally is known as the "Bean Belt," stretching horizontally across the equator between the Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn.

Ethiopia is widely recognized as the genetic birthplace of Arabica coffee, with early legends tracing its discovery to a 9th-century goat herder named Kaldi.

Brazil has remained the world's largest exporter of raw green coffee beans for over 150 years, producing millions of bags annually.

After harvesting, beans undergo processing to remove the fruit; the washed process produces a clean, bright cup, while the natural dry process yields heavy, fruit-forward flavors.

Roasting causes chemical changes such as the Maillard reaction and caramelization, which develop the complex aroma compounds inside the bean.

During roasting, a popping sound called the "first crack" occurs around 196°C to 205°C, marking the transition into light-to-medium roast profiles.

For manual pour-over brewing, water temperature should ideally sit between 90°C and 96°C to avoid under-extracting or scalding the grounds.

Using a burr grinder instead of a blade grinder ensures uniform particle size, preventing channelized water flow through the coffee bed.

The French press is a classic immersion brewing method that typically requires a coarse grind size and a steep time of four minutes.

Espresso uses finely ground coffee packed into a tight puck, where hot water is forced through under roughly 9 bars of pressure for 25 to 30 seconds.

Storing roasted beans in an airtight, opaque container away from direct sunlight, heat, and moisture prevents rapid oxidation and staleness.

Darker roasts contain slightly less bean mass and moisture than light roasts due to longer heat exposure, though caffeine levels per bean remain nearly identical.
